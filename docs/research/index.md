
# Research Overview

## Background and Motivation

Conventional Coverage Path Planning (CPP) algorithms
typically prioritise complete coverage and travel distance.

However, vertical glass-climbing robots experience
additional energy requirements associated with gravity,
continuous suction adhesion, turning and cleaning.

Consequently, the shortest coverage path may not
necessarily be the most energy-efficient path.

## Problem Statement

How can a low-cost window-cleaning robot minimise
energy consumption while achieving complete coverage
of a vertical glass facade?

## Research Questions

1. How do different coverage patterns affect energy
   consumption on a vertical surface?

2. Which path characteristics have the greatest
   influence on energy consumption?

3. Can an energy-aware coverage planner outperform
   conventional strategies while maintaining comparable
   coverage and tracking accuracy?

## Research Scope

The initial investigation assumes:

- Flat vertical glass
- Rectangular cleaning region
- No obstacles
- Known starting position
- Suction-based adhesion
- Low-cost motors, sensors and controller

## Proposed Methodology

1. Literature review
2. Define the operating scenario
3. Develop the robot prototype
4. Characterise energy consumption
5. Develop the mathematical energy model
6. Implement CPP simulation
7. Compare candidate coverage paths
8. Develop an energy-aware planner
9. Conduct experimental validation
10. Analyse results
